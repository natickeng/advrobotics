# Part 1: How it works

## Build from your model, not from memory

By the time you start this project, every decision about what goes where has already been made — in Project 01. This project is about executing that plan correctly with real parts, real bolts, and real wires. If you get partway through and realize you want to do something different than your OnShape model shows, that's a sign to go back and update the model first, not to freelance the physical build. Keeping the model and the real robot in sync is what makes the model worth having.

## Wiring matters as much as assembly

A squarebot that's mechanically perfect but wired wrong won't run, and wired wrong in the worst case damages the controller. A few rules that are non-negotiable, not stylistic:

- **Powerpole connectors: red-to-red, black-to-black. Always.** Getting this backwards on a motor or battery connection is how controllers get damaged.
- **Servo wire orientation matters** if you're using any servos later — the black (ground) wire faces the notched side of the port.
- **Encoder ports are keyed 4-pin connectors, separate from the 3-pin sensor ports.** Motor Port 1 is hardwired to Encoder Port 1, and Motor Port 2 to Encoder Port 2 — that pairing isn't something you configure, it's fixed in the hardware, and it's why your two front wheels (the ones on the main PRIZM controller) are the ones with real encoder feedback later.
- **Only the TETRIX 12V NiMH battery pack.** No substitutes, and never two packs paralleled into one controller.

## The I2C expansion module

If your drivetrain is four mecanum wheels (per Project 01), your back two wheels are driven by a PRIZM DC Expansion module chained onto the main controller over I2C, not by the main controller directly. That module needs its own I2C address, set correctly before you wire it in — mismatched or duplicate addresses are the most common reason an expansion module doesn't respond.

# Part 2: Do the work

## Step 1: Assemble the frame

Build the rail frame exactly as your OnShape model shows it. If something doesn't fit the way the model said it would, stop and figure out why before forcing it — a part that doesn't fit is telling you something about either the model or your build, and it's worth knowing which before you bolt around the problem.

## Step 2: Mount motors and wheels

Attach the motors and mecanum wheels in the positions and orientations your model specifies. Double-check wheel handedness again here — it's easy to get right in a model and still install the physical wheel backwards.

## Step 3: Wire the drivetrain

Connect your two front motors to the PRIZM's Motor Ports 1-2, observing Powerpole polarity. If you have an I2C expansion module for the back two motors, set its address and daisy-chain it onto the PRIZM's I2C expansion port.

## Step 4: Mount the battery and controller

Install the PRIZM controller, expansion module (if used), and battery in the positions your model shows. Route wiring so nothing is stretched taut or pinched under a moving part.

## Step 5: Power-on check

Before you demo it: connect the battery, confirm the PRIZM controller powers on normally, and visually confirm nothing is loose, pinched, or backwards. You are not running any code yet — this step is purely "does the hardware look right and safe."
